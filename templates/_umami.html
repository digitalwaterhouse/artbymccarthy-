{# ------------------------------------------------------------------- umami A visitor count that does not need to ask. WHY THIS SITS OUTSIDE THE CONSENT BAR while Google's tag sits inside it, and the distinction is the entire point of adding it: Umami sets no cookie and stores nothing on the device. ePrivacy consent is about reading from or writing to a visitor's machine, so with nothing stored there is nothing to ask about -- which is why this script can run for everyone, including the people who said "No thanks" to Google and the people who never answered. That is the gap it fills. The consent bar means Google only ever counted the share of visitors who pressed Allow, and those are not a random sample, so the number was both smaller than the truth and bent in a direction nobody could measure. This counts the rest. IT ALSO ONLY COUNTS REAL BROWSERS, which matters more than it sounds. A crawler fetches the page and leaves without running any of this, so it never registers -- the mistake the whales app made was counting page requests instead, and 1,440 "visitors" turned out to be about one person and a lot of datacentre robots. The id is a setting, pasted in the studio -- nothing here names an account, and taking it off the site is emptying one box rather than a deploy. It must be the website id Umami shows on the site's Settings page, not the domain. Self-hosting later changes only the src below; the id keeps working. NOT a replacement for Google's tag. Both can run; they answer different questions and disagreeing totals are expected, not a fault. #}
